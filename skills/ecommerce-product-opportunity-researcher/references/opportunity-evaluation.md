# Product Opportunity Evaluation

## Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer several independent signals.

1. Direct customer evidence, including purchases, preorders, surveys, support tickets, returns, and repeated review themes.
2. Marketplace evidence, including listing counts, bestseller movement, price distribution, review velocity, stock patterns, and listing quality.
3. Search evidence, including query volume, direction, seasonality, related searches, and commercial intent.
4. Community evidence, including recurring questions and complaints in forums, groups, videos, and comments.
5. Supplier evidence, including quotes, minimum orders, sample quality, lead times, defect policies, and certifications.
6. Competitor evidence, including assortment gaps, weak positioning, poor reviews, unavailable bundles, and unserved audiences.

Do not treat social virality by itself as durable demand.

## Scoring Rubric

Score every factor from one to five. Explain material uncertainty.

| Factor | Weight | Strong Score |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Demand strength. | 20. | Several recent signals show purchase intent or a recurring problem. |
| Competition opportunity. | 15. | Existing offers are weak, fragmented, poorly positioned, or fail a clear segment. |
| Margin potential. | 15. | Likely landed cost leaves room for fees, marketing, returns, and profit. |
| Differentiation. | 15. | The offer can improve meaningfully without relying on easy-to-copy styling. |
| Sourcing feasibility. | 10. | Samples, quality control, minimum orders, lead times, and backup suppliers are manageable. |
| Logistics fit. | 10. | The product is compact, durable, stable, and inexpensive to fulfill. |
| Brand and audience fit. | 5. | The product strengthens the catalog and matches customer expectations. |
| Repeat or expansion value. | 5. | Refills, accessories, bundles, variants, or adjacent products are credible. |
| Risk profile. | 5. | Compliance, safety, intellectual-property, return, and platform risks are limited. |

Calculate the weighted total on a 100-point scale.

Treat the score as a comparison tool, not proof of commercial success.

## Unit Economics

Use.

`Contribution per unit = selling price - product cost - inbound freight - duty - packaging - fulfillment - marketplace or payment fees - expected returns - advertising allowance.`

Show a range when costs remain uncertain. Separate gross margin from contribution margin. Identify unknown costs.

## Confidence

- High confidence. Several current and independent sources agree, and important cost inputs are known.
- Medium confidence. Evidence is promising, but one or more material assumptions remain.
- Low confidence. The idea remains a hypothesis, evidence is thin, or costs and restrictions are unclear.

## Minimum Validation Plan

For each shortlisted idea, specify.

- The assumption being tested.
- The cheapest credible test.
- The success threshold.
- The estimated time and budget.
- The next decision, such as proceed, revise, or reject.

Use small tests before large inventory commitments whenever the business model allows it.
